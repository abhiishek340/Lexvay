# COUN 710 Theories of Counseling and Psychotherapy
Fictional. Prototype data. Northfield University does not exist.
Northfield University, M.S. in Clinical Mental Health Counseling, 2026-27. 3 semester credits. Required course.

## Course description
This course surveys the major theories of counseling and psychotherapy and the research on how people change.
Students read the original sources, compare how each theory understands a client's difficulty and the helping relationship, and begin to build a personal theoretical orientation that fits their values and the evidence.

## Learning outcomes
Compare psychodynamic, cognitive behavioral, humanistic, existential and postmodern theories of counseling.
Explain the common factors that research links to good outcomes in every approach.
Write a clear statement of a personal theoretical orientation and support it with sources.

## Weekly schedule
Week 1 What a theory is for. The common factors: alliance, expectancy, empathy and feedback. Overview of outcome research. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 2 Psychoanalytic and psychodynamic theories. Unconscious process, defense, transference and the object relations tradition. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 3 Adlerian and brief dynamic approaches. Early recollections, lifestyle assessment and encouragement. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 4 Person centered theory. Unconditional positive regard, congruence, and the evidence for the core conditions. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 5 Existential and logotherapy approaches. Meaning, freedom, isolation and death as themes in the consulting room. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 6 Gestalt and experiential therapies. Awareness, contact and the use of experiments in session. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 7 Midterm examination and first practice demonstration. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 8 Behavioral therapy. Conditioning, exposure, activation and the origins of the behavioral tradition. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 9 Cognitive behavioral therapy. Cognitive models, thought records, behavioral experiments and relapse prevention. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 10 Acceptance and mindfulness based approaches. Values, defusion and committed action. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 11 Solution focused and narrative therapies. Exceptions, scaling, externalizing and re-authoring. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 12 Feminist and multicultural critiques of the major theories. Integration and eclecticism. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 13 Student demonstrations of one technique from the theory they find most useful, with a short critique from peers. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 14 Review. Personal theoretical orientation papers due. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.

## Assignments and assessment
Theory comparison paper (25%). A paper of 2,500 words comparing two theories on their view of the person, the cause of difficulty, the role of the counselor and the evidence of effectiveness.

Personal theoretical orientation paper (30%). A statement of the approach the student expects to use, how it fits their values, how it would be adapted for clients of different backgrounds, and what research supports it.

Technique demonstration (15%). A ten minute demonstration with a classmate, followed by peer feedback.

Examinations (20%). A midterm and a final of short essays.

Participation (10%).

Texts. Corsini and Wedding style survey text chosen by the instructor, plus primary sources posted to the learning site.

## University and program policies
Academic integrity. Students are expected to submit original work and to credit the ideas of others. Plagiarism, fabrication of data, unauthorized collaboration, and the submission of the same work in more than one course are violations of the Northfield University academic integrity policy. Violations are reported to the program director and may result in a failing grade for the assignment or the course. Students who are unsure what counts as appropriate collaboration should ask the instructor before they submit.

Accommodations. Students with documented disabilities, including learning, physical, sensory and mental health conditions, may request reasonable accommodations through the Office of Accessibility Services. Students should request accommodations in the first two weeks of the term so that arrangements can be made. Instructors cannot grant accommodations without a letter from that office, and they keep all such information confidential.

Grading scale. A 93-100, A- 90-92, B+ 87-89, B 83-86, B- 80-82, C+ 77-79, C 73-76, C- 70-72, F below 70. Graduate students must keep a cumulative grade point average of 3.0 to remain in good standing. A grade below B- in a core course must be repeated.

Attendance and participation. Students are expected to attend every class session and to take part in discussion. More than two unexcused absences may lower the final grade by one step. Students who must miss class should tell the instructor in advance.

Professional dispositions. Counseling students are expected to show respect for clients and colleagues, to keep client information confidential, to accept feedback, and to act in line with the professional ethics of the counseling profession in the classroom and at clinical sites. Concerns about a student's dispositions are reviewed by the program faculty under the program's professional dispositions policy.

Nondiscrimination. Northfield University does not discriminate on the basis of race, color, national origin, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, religion, age, disability or veteran status. Reports of discrimination or harassment may be made to the Title IX coordinator.
