# COUN 705 Multicultural Counseling and Social Justice
Fictional. Prototype data. Northfield University does not exist.
Northfield University, M.S. in Clinical Mental Health Counseling, 2026-27. 3 semester credits. Required course.

## Course description
This course prepares students to counsel clients across cultures and to advocate for social justice on behalf of the people they serve.
Students examine their own cultural identities, the history of the counseling profession's response to difference, and the ways that discrimination shapes mental health.
Class sessions combine lecture, small group discussion, community visits and practice with interpreters.

## Learning outcomes
Describe the cultural, racial, ethnic, religious and economic contexts that shape a client's experience.
Apply multicultural counseling competencies in a recorded practice session.
Plan an advocacy project that addresses a barrier to services in a local community.

## Weekly schedule
Week 1 Introductions and course agreements. Culture, identity and the many ways people describe themselves. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 2 The cultural assumptions of counseling theories. Where individualism helps a client and where it does not. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 3 Privilege, oppression and microaggressions. Students begin a reflective journal that continues all term. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 4 Race and ethnicity in the counseling relationship. Guest panel from a community health center. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 5 Multicultural counseling competencies and cultural humility. Practice with the competency self assessment. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 6 Religion and spirituality. Immigrant, refugee and asylum seeking clients and the effects of displacement. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 7 Socioeconomic status and poverty. How cost, transport and work schedules limit who can attend counseling. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 8 Midterm reflection due. Working with interpreters and with clients who speak limited English. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 9 Gender, sexual orientation and gender identity in clinical practice. Affirming approaches. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 10 Disability, aging and the intersections of identity. Accessibility of counseling spaces. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 11 Indigenous healing traditions, collaboration with traditional healers, and the history of research harms. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 12 Social justice counseling: from individual change to systems change. Models of advocacy. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 13 Student advocacy project presentations. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 14 Course review, final reflection and closing circle. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.

## Assignments and assessment
Reflective journal (25%). Weekly entries of about 400 words on readings and on the student's own cultural experiences. Entries are read by the instructor only.

Community learning visit (20%). Students visit a community organization approved by the instructor and write a report on the services it provides and the barriers its clients describe.

Recorded practice session (25%). A twenty minute role play with a classmate who plays a client from a background different from the student's own. The student reviews the recording against the competency checklist and writes a self evaluation.

Advocacy project (30%). A plan, with a timeline, for addressing one barrier to mental health services. Projects are presented in week 13.

Texts. A multicultural counseling textbook chosen by the instructor and articles posted on the learning site.

## University and program policies
Academic integrity. Students are expected to submit original work and to credit the ideas of others. Plagiarism, fabrication of data, unauthorized collaboration, and the submission of the same work in more than one course are violations of the Northfield University academic integrity policy. Violations are reported to the program director and may result in a failing grade for the assignment or the course. Students who are unsure what counts as appropriate collaboration should ask the instructor before they submit.

Accommodations. Students with documented disabilities, including learning, physical, sensory and mental health conditions, may request reasonable accommodations through the Office of Accessibility Services. Students should request accommodations in the first two weeks of the term so that arrangements can be made. Instructors cannot grant accommodations without a letter from that office, and they keep all such information confidential.

Grading scale. A 93-100, A- 90-92, B+ 87-89, B 83-86, B- 80-82, C+ 77-79, C 73-76, C- 70-72, F below 70. Graduate students must keep a cumulative grade point average of 3.0 to remain in good standing. A grade below B- in a core course must be repeated.

Attendance and participation. Students are expected to attend every class session and to take part in discussion. More than two unexcused absences may lower the final grade by one step. Students who must miss class should tell the instructor in advance.

Professional dispositions. Counseling students are expected to show respect for clients and colleagues, to keep client information confidential, to accept feedback, and to act in line with the professional ethics of the counseling profession in the classroom and at clinical sites. Concerns about a student's dispositions are reviewed by the program faculty under the program's professional dispositions policy.

Nondiscrimination. Northfield University does not discriminate on the basis of race, color, national origin, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, religion, age, disability or veteran status. Reports of discrimination or harassment may be made to the Title IX coordinator.
