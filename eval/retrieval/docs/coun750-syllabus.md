# COUN 750 Remote and Online Counseling Practice
Fictional. Prototype data. Northfield University does not exist.
Northfield University, M.S. in Clinical Mental Health Counseling, 2026-27. 3 semester credits. Elective course.

## Course description
Students learn to deliver counseling sessions through secure videoconferencing, to manage a remote crisis, and to protect client privacy online.
The course combines the evidence on remote delivery with hands-on practice on the training platform and covers the practical matters a new counselor meets in an online practice: setting, consent, backup plans and the law of the client's location.

## Learning outcomes
Conduct a counseling session through a secure videoconferencing platform.
Prepare an informed consent document for remote services, with a plan for technical failure and for emergencies.
Evaluate a platform for privacy and security before using it with clients.

## Weekly schedule
Week 1 The case for and against remote delivery. Research on outcomes of online sessions compared with in person work. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 2 Who benefits and who does not. Access, broadband, disability and rural clients. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 3 Platforms and privacy. Encryption, business agreements, recordings and the risks of consumer applications. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 4 Consent for remote services. Identity checks, location of the client, emergency contacts and what to do if the connection fails. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 5 Setting up the room. Camera, light, sound, background and the client's own environment. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 6 Building the alliance through a screen. Presence, silence, eye contact and reading the body in a small frame. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 7 Midterm practice session on the training platform with a standardized client. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 8 Remote crisis management. Verifying the client's address, involving local responders and documenting the steps taken. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 9 Practicing across state lines. Licensing of the counselor and the law of the client's home state. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 10 Working with couples, families and groups online. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 11 Asynchronous contact. Messages, email, text reminders and the boundaries they require. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 12 Social media, search results and the counselor's digital footprint. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 13 Emerging tools. Applications, self help programs and automated tools and how counselors should respond to clients who use them. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 14 Final practice session and course review. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.

## Assignments and assessment
Platform review (20%). A written review of one videoconferencing platform against a privacy and security checklist.

Consent document (20%). A complete consent form for remote services with an emergency plan.

Practice sessions (35%). Two recorded sessions on the training platform, the first in week 7 and the second in week 14, each with a self review.

Quizzes (15%) and participation (10%).

Texts. Selected chapters and articles on remote practice, posted on the learning site.

## University and program policies
Academic integrity. Students are expected to submit original work and to credit the ideas of others. Plagiarism, fabrication of data, unauthorized collaboration, and the submission of the same work in more than one course are violations of the Northfield University academic integrity policy. Violations are reported to the program director and may result in a failing grade for the assignment or the course. Students who are unsure what counts as appropriate collaboration should ask the instructor before they submit.

Accommodations. Students with documented disabilities, including learning, physical, sensory and mental health conditions, may request reasonable accommodations through the Office of Accessibility Services. Students should request accommodations in the first two weeks of the term so that arrangements can be made. Instructors cannot grant accommodations without a letter from that office, and they keep all such information confidential.

Grading scale. A 93-100, A- 90-92, B+ 87-89, B 83-86, B- 80-82, C+ 77-79, C 73-76, C- 70-72, F below 70. Graduate students must keep a cumulative grade point average of 3.0 to remain in good standing. A grade below B- in a core course must be repeated.

Attendance and participation. Students are expected to attend every class session and to take part in discussion. More than two unexcused absences may lower the final grade by one step. Students who must miss class should tell the instructor in advance.

Professional dispositions. Counseling students are expected to show respect for clients and colleagues, to keep client information confidential, to accept feedback, and to act in line with the professional ethics of the counseling profession in the classroom and at clinical sites. Concerns about a student's dispositions are reviewed by the program faculty under the program's professional dispositions policy.

Nondiscrimination. Northfield University does not discriminate on the basis of race, color, national origin, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, religion, age, disability or veteran status. Reports of discrimination or harassment may be made to the Title IX coordinator.
