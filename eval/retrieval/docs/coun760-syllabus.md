# COUN 760 Statistics for Behavioral Research
Fictional. Prototype data. Northfield University does not exist.
Northfield University, M.S. in Clinical Mental Health Counseling, 2026-27. 3 semester credits. Elective course.

## Course description
An applied course in the statistical methods used in counseling research. Students learn to describe data, to test hypotheses and to read published studies critically.
No advanced mathematics is assumed. Students use a statistical software package in a weekly computer laboratory.

## Learning outcomes
Choose and compute an appropriate statistic for a research question with one or two variables.
Interpret effect sizes, confidence intervals and the results of significance tests.
Judge the quality of a quantitative study reported in a journal.

## Weekly schedule
Week 1 Why counselors need statistics. Variables, scales of measurement and the logic of sampling. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 2 Describing data. Tables, graphs, the mean, the median and measures of spread. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 3 The normal distribution, standard scores and probability. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 4 Sampling distributions and the central limit theorem. Confidence intervals. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 5 Descriptive statistics for two variables: correlation, regression and their limits. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 6 Hypothesis testing. Null and alternative hypotheses, errors and power. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 7 Midterm examination. Computer laboratory on data entry and cleaning. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 8 Comparing two means. Independent and paired t tests, effect size and assumptions. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 9 Descriptive statistics for several groups: analysis of variance and post hoc comparisons. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 10 Factorial designs and interaction effects. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 11 Chi square tests and statistics for categorical data. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 12 Nonparametric alternatives and when to use them. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 13 Reliability and validity statistics. Reading a published psychometric study. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 14 Review, critique of a published article and final examination. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.

## Assignments and assessment
Laboratory exercises (30%). Eleven short exercises using the statistical package, each with a one paragraph interpretation.

Article critique (20%). A critique of a quantitative study from a counseling journal, covering design, analysis, conclusions and ethics.

Data analysis project (25%). An analysis of a provided data set with a written report in the style of a journal article.

Examinations (20%) and participation (5%).

Texts. A statistics textbook for the behavioral sciences chosen by the instructor and a statistical software package available in the university laboratories.

## University and program policies
Academic integrity. Students are expected to submit original work and to credit the ideas of others. Attendance and participation. Students are expected to attend every class session and to take part in discussion. More than two unexcused absences may lower the final grade by one step. Students who must miss class should tell the instructor in advance.

Professional dispositions. Counseling students are expected to show respect for clients and colleagues, to keep client information confidential, to accept feedback, and to act in line with the professional ethics of the counseling profession in the classroom and at clinical sites. Concerns about a student's dispositions are reviewed by the program faculty under the program's professional dispositions policy.
