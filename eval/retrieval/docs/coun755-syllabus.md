# COUN 755 Consultation and Advocacy in Schools and Communities
Fictional. Prototype data. Northfield University does not exist.
Northfield University, M.S. in Clinical Mental Health Counseling, 2026-27. 3 semester credits. Elective course.

## Course description
Counselors often work through other people: teachers, physicians, employers, agencies and families.
This course teaches the models of consultation, the skills of collaboration and the practice of advocacy at the level of a school, an agency and a community.
Students complete a consultation project with a community partner.

## Learning outcomes
Describe the major models of consultation and choose one for a given request.
Conduct a consultation interview and write a consultation plan.
Design an outreach or prevention program for a community group.

## Weekly schedule
Week 1 What consultation is and how it differs from counseling and supervision. The triadic relationship. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 2 Entry into a system. Contracting, expectations, confidentiality and the question of who the client is. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 3 Understanding organizations. Culture, structure, power and readiness for change. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 4 Communication skills for consultants. Listening, questioning, feedback and presenting findings. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 5 Needs assessment. Surveys, interviews, focus groups and the use of existing data. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 6 Program planning and logic models. Setting goals and choosing measures. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 7 Midterm examination and project proposals due. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 8 Consultation models: mental health, behavioral and process consultation, with a practice case for each. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 9 School consultation. Working with teachers, administrators and student support teams. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 10 Consultation with health care, employers and agencies. Working in an interdisciplinary team. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 11 Advocacy in practice. Client advocacy, school and community advocacy and public policy. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 12 Prevention and outreach. Psychoeducation, screening events and community partnerships. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 13 Resistance, conflict and ethical problems in consultation. Dual roles. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 14 Project presentations and review. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.

## Assignments and assessment
Consultation project (40%). Students work with a community partner for ten weeks to identify a need and write a plan. The final report includes the contract, the assessment, the plan and a reflection.

Outreach program design (25%). A complete program with goals, activities, schedule and evaluation measures.

Case write ups (15%). Three short analyses of consultation cases.

Examinations (10%) and participation (10%).

Texts. A consultation textbook chosen by the instructor.

## University and program policies
Academic integrity. Students are expected to submit original work and to credit the ideas of others. Plagiarism, fabrication of data, unauthorized collaboration, and the submission of the same work in more than one course are violations of the Northfield University academic integrity policy. Violations are reported to the program director and may result in a failing grade for the assignment or the course. Accommodations. Students with documented disabilities, including learning, physical, sensory and mental health conditions, may request reasonable accommodations through the Office of Accessibility Services. Students should request accommodations in the first two weeks of the term so that arrangements can be made. Grading scale. A 93-100, A- 90-92, B+ 87-89, B 83-86, B- 80-82, C+ 77-79, C 73-76, C- 70-72, F below 70. Graduate students must keep a cumulative grade point average of 3.0 to remain in good standing. A grade below B- in a core course must be repeated.

Attendance and participation. Students are expected to attend every class session and to take part in discussion. More than two unexcused absences may lower the final grade by one step. Students who must miss class should tell the instructor in advance.

Professional dispositions. Counseling students are expected to show respect for clients and colleagues, to keep client information confidential, to accept feedback, and to act in line with the professional ethics of the counseling profession in the classroom and at clinical sites. Concerns about a student's dispositions are reviewed by the program faculty under the program's professional dispositions policy.
