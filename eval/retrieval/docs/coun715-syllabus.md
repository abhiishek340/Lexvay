# COUN 715 Family Systems Therapy
Fictional. Prototype data. Northfield University does not exist.
Northfield University, M.S. in Clinical Mental Health Counseling, 2026-27. 3 semester credits. Required course.

## Course description
Students learn systemic therapy with couples, parents and children, and extended kin.
The course treats the family as an emotional unit and asks how patterns of communication, loyalty and power sustain or relieve a presenting problem.
Students watch recorded sessions, practice with genograms and conduct a mock first interview with a family.

## Learning outcomes
Explain the major theories of family systems and the assumptions they share.
Draw and interpret a three generation genogram.
Plan a first session with a couple or a family, including who attends and how consent is handled.

## Weekly schedule
Week 1 Systems thinking. Circular causality, homeostasis and the identified patient. A brief history of the field. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 2 The family life cycle and the stress of transitions. Single parent, blended, adoptive, multigenerational and chosen families. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 3 Assessment of families. Genograms, ecomaps and the first interview. Practice with a case from the course packet. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 4 Communication approaches. Double binds, congruent communication and the use of reframing. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 5 Structural family therapy. Boundaries, subsystems, hierarchy, enactment and joining. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 6 Bowen family systems theory. Differentiation of self, emotional triangles, and multigenerational transmission of patterns. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 7 Midterm examination. Genogram presentations in small groups. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 8 Strategic and brief approaches. Directives, paradox and the mental research institute tradition. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 9 Experiential and attachment based approaches with couples. Emotional cycles and softening. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 10 Narrative and collaborative approaches. Language, stories and the therapist as a curious guest. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 11 Intimate partner violence, safety screening and the limits of conjoint work. Mandated reporting duties. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 12 Divorce, separation, remarriage and the children of changing households. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 13 Culture, class and sexuality in systemic work. Student role plays of a first session. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 14 Integration and final examination. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.

## Assignments and assessment
Genogram project (20%). A three generation genogram of a family approved by the instructor, which may be a fictional family or the student's own, with a two page interpretation. Students are not required to disclose their own family history.

First session plan (25%). A written plan for the first meeting with a couple or family, covering who is invited, consent, confidentiality when several people are present, goals for the hour and possible difficulties.

Role play (20%). A recorded fifteen minute mock session with classmates who play family members, with a written self review.

Examinations (25%). Midterm and final.

Participation (10%).

Texts. A systemic therapy textbook chosen by the instructor and the case packet.
