# COUN 740 Human Sexuality in Counseling
Fictional. Prototype data. Northfield University does not exist.
Northfield University, M.S. in Clinical Mental Health Counseling, 2026-27. 3 semester credits. Elective course.

## Course description
Students examine human sexual development, sexual health and sexual difficulties, and learn to talk about them with clients without embarrassment or bias.
The course is academic and clinical in tone. Students are never asked to disclose personal sexual history, and classroom conduct follows the program's professional dispositions policy.

## Learning outcomes
Describe sexual development and function across the lifespan.
Take a sexual history in a respectful, clinically useful way.
Identify when to refer a client to a medical provider or a specialist.

## Weekly schedule
Week 1 Attitudes and values. Why counselors avoid the topic and what clients lose when they do. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 2 The sexual response and the biology of desire. Hormones, medications and illness. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 3 Gender identity and sexual orientation across the lifespan. Development, coming out and affirming practice. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 4 Sexual development in childhood and adolescence. Sex education, consent and online life. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 5 Sexual health. Contraception, infection prevention and reproductive decisions. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 6 Taking a sexual history. Language, pacing and the use of a structured interview. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 7 Midterm examination. Practice interviews with a standardized client. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 8 Sexual difficulties in adults. Desire, arousal, orgasm and pain, and the role of the relationship. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 9 Sexuality after trauma, illness or injury. Collaboration with medical providers. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 10 Sexuality, aging and disability. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 11 Sexual behavior outside the usual range. Distinguishing difference from disorder, and what the law requires counselors to report. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 12 Sexual violence, harassment and exploitation. Support for survivors and the limits of counseling. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 13 Culture, religion and sexuality. Working with clients whose values differ from the counselor's. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 14 Review and final examination. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.

## Assignments and assessment
Sexual history interview (25%). A recorded interview with a standardized client, with a written self evaluation.

Case analysis (30%). A written analysis of a case in which a sexual concern is part of the presenting problem, including a referral plan.

Resource guide (15%). A guide for clients on a topic of the student's choosing, written at an eighth grade reading level and reviewed by the instructor.

Examinations (20%) and participation (10%).

Texts. A text in counseling and human sexuality chosen by the instructor.

## University and program policies
Academic integrity. Students are expected to submit original work and to credit the ideas of others. Plagiarism, fabrication of data, unauthorized collaboration, and the submission of the same work in more than one course are violations of the Northfield University academic integrity policy. Violations are reported to the program director and may result in a failing grade for the assignment or the course. Students who are unsure what counts as appropriate collaboration should ask the instructor before they submit.

Accommodations. Students with documented disabilities, including learning, physical, sensory and mental health conditions, may request reasonable accommodations through the Office of Accessibility Services. Students should request accommodations in the first two weeks of the term so that arrangements can be made. Instructors cannot grant accommodations without a letter from that office, and they keep all such information confidential.

Grading scale. A 93-100, A- 90-92, B+ 87-89, B 83-86, B- 80-82, C+ 77-79, C 73-76, C- 70-72, F below 70. Graduate students must keep a cumulative grade point average of 3.0 to remain in good standing. A grade below B- in a core course must be repeated.

Attendance and participation. Students are expected to attend every class session and to take part in discussion. More than two unexcused absences may lower the final grade by one step. Students who must miss class should tell the instructor in advance.

Professional dispositions. Counseling students are expected to show respect for clients and colleagues, to keep client information confidential, to accept feedback, and to act in line with the professional ethics of the counseling profession in the classroom and at clinical sites. Concerns about a student's dispositions are reviewed by the program faculty under the program's professional dispositions policy.

Nondiscrimination. Northfield University does not discriminate on the basis of race, color, national origin, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, religion, age, disability or veteran status. Reports of discrimination or harassment may be made to the Title IX coordinator.
