# COUN 701 Psychopathology and Abnormal Behavior
Fictional. Prototype data. Northfield University does not exist.
Northfield University, M.S. in Clinical Mental Health Counseling, 2026-27. 3 semester credits. Required course.

## Course description
Students study psychopathology, the classification of psychiatric syndromes, and the biological, psychological and social origins of abnormal behavior.
The course follows the major categories of the current diagnostic manual and asks students to read case material critically, noting where a syndrome description fits a person poorly.
It is a companion to COUN 615 and assumes no prior coursework in the biological sciences.

## Learning outcomes
Describe the presentation, course and prevalence of mood, anxiety, psychotic, personality and neurodevelopmental syndromes.
Explain how genetic, neurological, developmental and cultural factors contribute to the onset of abnormal behavior.
Distinguish a syndrome from the ordinary range of human distress in written case formulations.

## Weekly schedule
Week 1 What is abnormal? Statistical, cultural and clinical definitions. A short history of the classification of psychiatric syndromes. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 2 Research on mental illness: prevalence, comorbidity, risk and protective factors, and the limits of case reports. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 3 Mood syndromes: major depression, persistent depressive presentations, bipolar spectrum, course and relapse. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 4 Neuroanatomy, neurotransmitter systems and brain function in mood and anxiety presentations. Guest lecture from a psychiatric nurse practitioner. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 5 Anxiety syndromes: panic, social anxiety, generalized worry, phobias, and the role of avoidance in keeping symptoms going. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 6 Obsessive and compulsive presentations, trauma and stressor related syndromes, and the overlap between them. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 7 Midterm examination. Debrief of case formulation drafts. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 8 Psychotic syndromes: schizophrenia spectrum, early signs, family response, and long-term outcomes. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 9 Personality syndromes: clusters, the dimensional alternative, and the stigma attached to the label. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 10 Neurodevelopmental syndromes: attention, autism spectrum, intellectual disability, and how they appear in adults. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 11 Eating, sleep, and substance related presentations. Medical conditions that mimic psychiatric syndromes. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 12 Culture and abnormal behavior: culture bound syndromes, idioms of distress, and bias in clinical descriptions. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 13 Resilience, recovery and the strengths perspective. Student presentations of case formulations. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 14 Review and final examination. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.

## Assignments and assessment
Case formulations (35%). Three written formulations of 1,200 words each, based on published case material. Each formulation describes the presenting syndrome, the factors that may have contributed to it, and the questions a counselor would still want answered. Formulations are graded on accuracy, on the quality of the reasoning, and on the care taken with language about people.

Midterm and final examinations (40%). Short answers and one extended essay each. The final examination covers the whole course.

Presentation (15%). A twelve minute presentation on one syndrome, including a short account of what the research does not yet explain.

Participation (10%). Preparation for class, contributions to discussion, and respectful engagement with classmates.

Required text. A current abnormal psychology textbook chosen by the instructor each year, and the course reader of journal articles posted on the learning site.

## University and program policies
Academic integrity. Students are expected to submit original work and to credit the ideas of others. Plagiarism, fabrication of data, unauthorized collaboration, and the submission of the same work in more than one course are violations of the Northfield University academic integrity policy. Violations are reported to the program director and may result in a failing grade for the assignment or the course. Students who are unsure what counts as appropriate collaboration should ask the instructor before they submit.

Accommodations. Students with documented disabilities, including learning, physical, sensory and mental health conditions, may request reasonable accommodations through the Office of Accessibility Services. Students should request accommodations in the first two weeks of the term so that arrangements can be made. Instructors cannot grant accommodations without a letter from that office, and they keep all such information confidential.

Grading scale. A 93-100, A- 90-92, B+ 87-89, B 83-86, B- 80-82, C+ 77-79, C 73-76, C- 70-72, F below 70. Graduate students must keep a cumulative grade point average of 3.0 to remain in good standing. A grade below B- in a core course must be repeated.

Attendance and participation. Students are expected to attend every class session and to take part in discussion. More than two unexcused absences may lower the final grade by one step. Students who must miss class should tell the instructor in advance.

Professional dispositions. Counseling students are expected to show respect for clients and colleagues, to keep client information confidential, to accept feedback, and to act in line with the professional ethics of the counseling profession in the classroom and at clinical sites. Concerns about a student's dispositions are reviewed by the program faculty under the program's professional dispositions policy.

Nondiscrimination. Northfield University does not discriminate on the basis of race, color, national origin, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, religion, age, disability or veteran status. Reports of discrimination or harassment may be made to the Title IX coordinator.
