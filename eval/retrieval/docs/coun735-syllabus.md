# COUN 735 Grief, Loss and Life Transitions
Fictional. Prototype data. Northfield University does not exist.
Northfield University, M.S. in Clinical Mental Health Counseling, 2026-27. 3 semester credits. Elective course.

## Course description
The course examines how people experience death, loss and major change, and how counselors can support them.
Students study theories of mourning, the course of normal and complicated grief, and rituals across cultures.
The course asks students to reflect on their own losses with care; the instructor will discuss options with any student who finds this difficult.

## Learning outcomes
Compare the major theories of grief and mourning and the evidence for each.
Distinguish expected grief from complicated loss and describe approaches to each.
Plan a support group or a ritual for people facing a loss.

## Weekly schedule
Week 1 Death in culture and in the counselor's own life. Course agreements about self care. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 2 Early theories of grief. Stages, tasks and the criticism of stage models. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 3 Continuing bonds, the dual process model and meaning reconstruction. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 4 Children and grief. What children understand at different ages and how to talk with them. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 5 Adolescents and young adults. Loss of a friend, a parent or a future. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 6 Older adults, widowhood and the losses of aging. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 7 Midterm examination and a visit from a hospice social worker. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 8 Grief after sudden, violent and ambiguous loss. Missing persons and disasters. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 9 Bereavement after a death by suicide. The needs of survivors and the stigma they face. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 10 Grief, bereavement and complicated loss: criteria, assessment and treatment of prolonged grief. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 11 Non death losses. Divorce, job loss, chronic illness, infertility and immigration. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 12 Rituals, religion and spirituality in mourning. Funeral practices in several traditions. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 13 Group and community responses. Planning a bereavement support group. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 14 Counselor self care and the review of the course. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.

## Assignments and assessment
Reflective journal (20%). Weekly entries that are read by the instructor only.

Theory paper (25%). A paper of 2,000 words comparing two grief theories and applying them to a case.

Support group or ritual plan (30%). A written plan, with materials, for a six session group or a memorial ritual for a stated community.

Examinations (15%) and participation (10%).

Texts. A grief counseling textbook chosen by the instructor and a reader of articles.

## University and program policies

Professional dispositions.
