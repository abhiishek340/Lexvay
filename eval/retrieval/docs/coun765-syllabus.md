# COUN 765 Professional Writing and Clinical Paperwork
Fictional. Prototype data. Northfield University does not exist.
Northfield University, M.S. in Clinical Mental Health Counseling, 2026-27. 3 semester credits. Elective course.

## Course description
Counselors write for many readers: supervisors, insurers, courts, schools and clients themselves.
This course teaches the progress notes, charting, treatment file retention practices and reports that a new clinician must produce, with attention to clarity, accuracy and privacy.
Students write every week and receive detailed feedback.

## Learning outcomes
Write a clear progress note, intake summary and treatment plan from a case study.
Describe how long clinical files must be kept and how they are stored and destroyed safely.
Respond to a request for client information, deciding what may be released and to whom.

## Weekly schedule
Week 1 Why clinical writing matters. Who reads what counselors write and what happens when notes are poor. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 2 Plain language and the facts of a session. Observation versus inference, and avoiding judgmental terms. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 3 Intake summaries. Presenting concerns, history, risk and the first impression. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 4 Progress notes. Formats, length and the habit of writing within a day of the session. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 5 Treatment plans. Problems, goals, objectives, interventions and measures of change. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 6 Charting for risk. How to write about safety concerns, consultation and decisions in a way that would hold up later. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 7 Midterm writing assessment. Peer review of progress notes. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 8 Privacy of the clinical file. Storage, electronic systems, sharing between providers and the rights of clients to see their own file. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 9 Retention and disposal. State rules on how long files are kept and what happens when a practice closes. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 10 Reports for outside readers. Insurers, schools, courts and employers, and the limits of what to say. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.
Week 11 Responding to subpoenas, court orders and requests from other professionals. When to seek legal advice. Reading: the assigned chapter and the case in the course packet. In class: case analysis in pairs, then a whole class debrief led by students.
Week 12 Writing letters and referrals. Letters of support, termination letters and transfer summaries. Reading: the assigned chapter of the course text and one article from the course reader. In class: lecture followed by small group discussion of a case.
Week 13 Writing about research and practice. Short professional articles and case study reports. Preparation: read the assigned chapter and post a short question on the learning site before class. In class: demonstration and guided practice in pairs.
Week 14 Portfolio review and course review. Reading: two articles from the course reader. In class: structured debate and a short reflection written before leaving.

## Assignments and assessment
Weekly writing assignments (40%). Notes, plans and short reports based on fictional case studies, graded on accuracy, clarity and tone.

Clinical file portfolio (30%). A complete file for a fictional client: intake, treatment plan, six progress notes and a termination summary.

Release of information exercise (15%). Written responses to five requests for client information, with reasons.

Midterm assessment (10%) and participation (5%).

Texts. A clinical writing guide chosen by the instructor and sample forms posted on the learning site.

## University and program policies
Academic integrity. Students are expected to submit original work and to credit the ideas of others. Plagiarism, fabrication of data, unauthorized collaboration, and the submission of the same work in more than one course are violations of the Northfield University academic integrity policy. Violations are reported to the program director and may result in a failing grade for the assignment or the course. Students who are unsure what counts as appropriate collaboration should ask the instructor before they submit.

Accommodations. Students with documented disabilities, including learning, physical, sensory and mental health conditions, may request reasonable accommodations through the Office of Accessibility Services. Students should request accommodations in the first two weeks of the term so that arrangements can be made. Instructors cannot grant accommodations without a letter from that office, and they keep all such information confidential.

Grading scale. A 93-100, A- 90-92, B+ 87-89, B 83-86, B- 80-82, C+ 77-79, C 73-76, C- 70-72, F below 70. Graduate students must keep a cumulative grade point average of 3.0 to remain in good standing. A grade below B- in a core course must be repeated.

Attendance and participation. Students are expected to attend every class session and to take part in discussion. More than two unexcused absences may lower the final grade by one step. Students who must miss class should tell the instructor in advance.

Professional dispositions. Counseling students are expected to show respect for clients and colleagues, to keep client information confidential, to accept feedback, and to act in line with the professional ethics of the counseling profession in the classroom and at clinical sites. Concerns about a student's dispositions are reviewed by the program faculty under the program's professional dispositions policy.

Nondiscrimination. Northfield University does not discriminate on the basis of race, color, national origin, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, religion, age, disability or veteran status. Reports of discrimination or harassment may be made to the Title IX coordinator.
